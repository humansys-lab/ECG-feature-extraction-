# Correspondence between the ECG waveform interpretation standards and the 12 leads

Based on the content of the "Classification of Various Waveforms" chart, this document supplements the actual interpretation methods, quantitative thresholds of each waveform, and the lead combinations that should be focused on in the 12-lead electrocardiogram for clinical/teaching reference.

> Note: The following thresholds are mainly compiled with reference to public clinical data such as LITFL (Life in the Fast Lane) ECG Library, StatPearls and AHA/ACC/HRS electrocardiogram standardization recommendations. There may be slight differences in specific values ​​in different textbooks/guidelines. For actual diagnosis, please refer to the guidelines adopted by your institution.

---

<a id="1-p波心房除极"></a>
## 1. P wave (atrial depolarization)

<a id="11-正常范围"></a>
### 1.1 Normal range

| Parameters | Normal values |
|---|---|
| Time limit | < 0.12 seconds (< 3 cells) |
| Amplitude (limb leads) | < 2.5 mm (0.25 mV) |
| Amplitude (chest leads) | < 1.5 mm (0.15 mV) |
| Electrical axis | 0°~ +75°, leads I and II upright, aVR inverted |
| Morphology | Lead II is unidirectional, lead V1 is bidirectional (positive first and then negative) |

<a id="12-异常分类与阈值"></a>
### 1.2 Anomaly classification and threshold

| Classification | Judgment Criteria | Common Causes |
|---|---|---|
| Pulmonary P wave (P pulmonale) | High peak P wave in leads II, III, and aVF, amplitude > 2.5 mm; starting forward wave in lead V1 > 1.5 mm | Right atrium enlargement (pulmonary hypertension, chronic pulmonary heart disease, etc.) |
| Mitral P wave (P mitrale) | P wave widening in lead II > 0.12 seconds, often bimodal (notch); terminal negative part in lead V1 widened > 40 ms and depth > 1 mm (ie Morris index / P terminal force > 0.04 mm·second) | Left atrial enlargement (mitral stenosis, etc.) |
| Bi-atrial enlargement | Meet the criteria for both left and right atrial enlargement | Mitral + tricuspid valve disease, mixed heart disease |
| P wave inversion | P wave inversion in inferior leads; PR interval < 120 ms indicates junctional origin, ≥ 120 ms indicates ectopic atrial origin | Atrial/junctional escape rhythm |
| Polygenic atrial rhythm/atrial tachycardia | ≥3 different forms of P waves (heart rate <100 is polygenic atrial rhythm, ≥100 is MAT) | Multiple atrial ectopic pacemaking points |

**Focus on the leads**: Inferior leads (II, III, aVF) and V1 - these two sets of leads have the most obvious P waves and are the most sensitive leads for judging atrial hypertrophy.

<a id="2-qrs波-q波心室除极"></a>
## 2. QRS wave / Q wave (ventricular depolarization)

<a id="21-命名规则复习"></a>
### 2.1 Naming rules (review)

- The first negative wave is **Q wave**; the first positive wave is **R wave**; the negative wave after the positive wave is **S wave**; the subsequent positive/negative waves are respectively recorded as **R' wave / S' wave**
- Capital letters for amplitude represent large amplitudes, and lowercase letters represent small amplitudes (such as qR, rS, rSR')

<a id="22-q波病理性判断阈值"></a>
### 2.2 Q wave pathological judgment threshold

Normal septal Q waves are common in the left leads (I, aVL, V5, V6), representing the normal electrical activity of the ventricular septum depolarizing from left to right.

| Criteria for determining **pathological Q wave** (any criterion is considered abnormal) |
|---|
| Time limit > 40 ms(1 mm) |
| Depth > 2 mm |
| Depth > 25% of the total amplitude of the same lead QRS wave |
| Appears in leads V1–V3 (Q waves should not normally appear in this area) |

- Deep Q wave (> 2mm) in lead III and aVR can be a normal variation
- Absence of Q waves in leads V5 and V6 should be considered abnormal and is common in left bundle branch block (LBBB)

**Focus on the leads (pathological Q wave localization of myocardial infarction area)**:

| Q wave occurrence lead | Prompt site |
|---|---|
| II, III, aVF | Inferior wall |
| V1–V4 | Anterior wall (V1-V2 interval, V3-V4 anterior wall) |
| I, aVL | High sidewalls |
| V5–V6 | Sidewall |

<a id="3-st段心室复极早期"></a>
## 3. ST segment (early ventricular repolarization)

<a id="31-j点与测量基准"></a>
### 3.1 J point and measurement datum

- **J point**: The intersection point between the end of the QRS wave and the beginning of the ST segment. It is the measurement position to determine the ST segment offset.
- Referring to the baseline, it is recommended to use **PQ junction (end of PR segment)** instead of TP segment (due to sinus tachycardia and other situations, the P wave may overlap with the T wave of the previous cardiac cycle, and the TP segment may disappear)
- Clinically, the degree of ST deviation is often evaluated 60 ms after the J point (i.e. "ST60") to avoid the influence of the changing shape of the J point itself.

<a id="32-st段抬高elevation阈值"></a>
### 3.2 ST segment elevation threshold

Taking the offset of the J point from the baseline as the judgment criterion, there are differences in lead, gender, and age:

| Lead | Population | ST elevation significance threshold |
|---|---|---|
| V2, V3 | Male < 40 years old | ≥ 0.25 mV(2.5 mm) |
| V2, V3 | Male ≥ 40 years old | ≥ 0.2 mV(2 mm) |
| V2, V3 | Females (regardless of age) | ≥ 0.15 mV (1.5 mm) |
| All remaining leads | Male/Female | ≥ 0.1 mV(1 mm) |

*(Data source: Normalized thresholds quoted in StatPearls "ST Segment" chapter)*

<a id="33-st段压低depression阈值"></a>
### 3.3 ST segment depression threshold

| Lead | Threshold |
|---|---|
| V2, V3 | ≥ 0.05 mV(0.5 mm) |
| All remaining leads | ≥ 0.1 mV(1 mm) |

Commonly used clinical standards (2007 ESC Task Force): **Horizontal or downsloping ST depression ≥ 0.5 mm, and ** must appear in ≥ 2 adjacent leads at the same time to have diagnostic significance for myocardial ischemia.

<a id="34-形态学分类与临床意义对应原图四种类型"></a>
### 3.4 Morphological classification and clinical significance (corresponding to the four types of original images)

| Morphology | Characteristics | Clinical significance |
|---|---|---|
| J type (early repolarization type) | J point elevation, ST segment then normal or slightly upward slope | Mostly benign early repolarization, common in young healthy people, may be accompanied by J point notch ("fish-hook" sign) |
| Slowly ascending type (SU type) | ST segment slowly slopes upward followed by normal T wave | Mostly normal variation or movement-related changes |
| Horizontal type (H type) | The ST segment is basically parallel to the baseline | Highly suggestive of myocardial ischemia, especially when accompanied by symmetrical inverted T waves |
| Descending slope type (DS type) | ST segment slopes downward from the J point | Highly specific for myocardial ischemia; also seen in digitalis effect ("fishhook-like") |

<a id="35-需重点关注的导联组合定位心肌缺血梗死区域"></a>
### 3.5 Lead combinations that need to be focused on (locating myocardial ischemia/infarction area)

| Anatomical parts | Lead combination | Corresponding blood vessels (common) |
|---|---|---|
| Inferior wall | II, III, aVF | The right coronary artery (RCA) is more common |
| Lateral wall/high lateral wall | I, aVL, V5, V6 | Left circumflex artery (LCX) |
| Anterior wall | V3, V4 | Left anterior descending artery (LAD) |
| Interval | V1, V2 | LAD near end |
| Right ventricle | V1, V4R | Proximal RCA |
| Back wall | V7–V9 (or V1-V3 as mirror image ST low + high R wave) | RCA/LCX |

- When judging STEMI, you should look for **mirror image ST depression in the corresponding lead** (mirror image depression often occurs in I and aVL when the inferior wall is elevated), which is helpful to distinguish true infarction from other causes of ST elevation (such as LVH, LBBB, pericarditis, Brugada, etc.).
- Extensive ST depression + ST elevation in lead aVR, suggesting possible left main trunk or three-vessel disease.

<a id="4-t波心室复极"></a>
## 4. T wave (ventricular repolarization)

<a id="41-正常范围"></a>
### 4.1 Normal range

| Parameters | Normal values |
|---|---|
| Orientation | Upright except aVR, V1 |
| Amplitude | Limb leads < 5 mm; Chest leads < 10 mm (upper limit for men: 10mm, upper limit for women: 8mm) |

<a id="42-异常分类"></a>
### 4.2 Exception classification

| Classification | Judgment Criteria | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| High-peak T wave | Narrow, symmetrical high-peak T wave | Hyperkalemia |
| Huge T wave (Hyperacute T wave) | Wide, asymmetrical and pointed, often significantly larger than QRS in amplitude | STEMI hyperacute phase (earlier than ST elevation/Q wave appearance) |
| T wave inversion (pathological) | Symmetrical and depth > 3 mm | Myocardial ischemia/infarction, conduction block, ventricular hypertrophy (strain type), pulmonary embolism, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, increased intracranial pressure, etc. |
| Huge negative T wave | Deep chest lead inversion (up to ≥ 10 mm) | Apical hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, etc. |
| Biphasic T wave | Positive first and then negative = ischemia; negative first and then positive = hypokalemia | Identify the cause according to the direction |
| QT prolongation with T wave changes | See Section 5 QTc standard | Non-Q wave infarction, intracranial hemorrhage, etc. |

*Note: T wave inversion in lead III is a normal variation; new T wave inversion (compared to previous ECG) should be considered abnormal. *

<a id="43-重点关注导联缺血定位与st段一致"></a>
### 4.3 Focus on the leads (ischemia localization, consistent with the ST segment)

| Site | Lead |
|---|---|
| Inferior wall | II, III, aVF |
| Sidewall | I, aVL, V5, V6 |
| Front wall | V2–V6 |

- **Dynamic** T wave inversion indicates acute ischemia; **Fixed** T wave inversion is often accompanied by pathological Q wave, indicating old infarction
- Wellens syndrome: biphasic or deep inverted T waves in leads V2–V3, indicating critical anterior descending artery disease (type A: positive and then negative biphasic; type B: deep symmetric inversion, usually evolved from type A)

<a id="5-qt间期-qtc校正qt间期"></a>
## 5. QT interval / QTc (corrected QT interval)

Although the original picture is not listed separately, QT/QTc is a key parameter for judging T wave and U wave abnormalities and repolarization delay, and is included together.

<a id="51-定义与测量"></a>
### 5.1 Definition and Measurement

- QT interval: from the beginning of QRS wave to the end of T wave
- It is recommended to use lead II or V5–V6 for measurement, and take the longest measurement value.
- If the U wave amplitude is > 1mm and merged with the T wave, it should be included in the measurement; the separated small U wave is not included in the measurement.

<a id="52-qtc正常范围与阈值"></a>
### 5.2 QTc normal range and threshold

| Judgment | Threshold |
|---|---|
| QTc extension | Male > 440 ms; Female > 460 ms |
| High risk (significantly increased risk of torsade de pointes) | QTc > 500 ms |
| QTc shortened | < 350 ms |

Commonly used correction formulas: Bazett (QTc = QT/√RR, most commonly used but overcorrected when the heart rate is fast), Fridericia (QTc = QT/RR^(1/3), more accurate when the heart rate is too fast or too slow).

<a id="6-u波"></a>
## 6. U wave

<a id="61-正常特征"></a>
### 6.1 Normal characteristics

| Parameters | Standard |
|---|---|
| Direction | Consistent with the direction of T wave in the same lead |
| Best observation leads | V2, V3 |
| Upper limit of normal amplitude | 1–2 mm, and < 25% of T wave amplitude in the same lead |
| Occurrence conditions | Easier to see when heart rate < 65 beats/minute (amplitude is inversely proportional to heart rate) |

<a id="62-异常分类"></a>
### 6.2 Exception classification

| Classification | Judgment Criteria | Common Causes |
|---|---|---|
| Prominent U wave (prominent) | Amplitude > 1–2 mm or > T wave amplitude 25% | Bradycardia, severe hypokalemia; also seen in hypocalcemia/hypomagnesemia, hypothermia, increased intracranial pressure, left ventricular hypertrophy, digoxin/quinidine/amiodarone and other drugs |
| U wave inversion (negative, corresponding to when T wave is upright) | Negative U wave appears in the upright T wave lead | Highly specific for myocardial ischemia, can be the earliest manifestation of unstable angina pectoris/early myocardial infarction; coronary heart disease, hypertension, valvular disease, etc. |

<a id="7-12导联分区速查表综合汇总"></a>
## 7. 12-Lead Zone Cheat Sheet (Comprehensive Summary)

Combined with the positioning rules of the above waveforms, it is recommended to simultaneously evaluate the ST segment, T wave, and Q wave changes in adjacent leads according to the following anatomical divisions when interpreting 12-lead ECG:

| Myocardial area | Lead combination | Main supply arteries |
|---|---|---|
| Inferior wall | II, III, aVF | Mainly right coronary artery (RCA) |
| High lateral wall | I, aVL | Left circumflex branch (LCX) |
| Lateral wall | V5, V6 | Left circumflex branch (LCX)/left anterior descending diagonal branch |
| Anteroseptal wall | V1, V2 | Proximal left anterior descending artery (LAD) |
| Anterior wall | V3, V4 | Left anterior descending artery (LAD) |
| Broad anterior wall | V1–V6(+I, aVL) | Proximal LAD/trunk |
| Right ventricle | V1, V4R (need to add right chest leads) | Proximal RCA |
| Posterior wall | V7–V9 (or V1-V3 mirror changes: ST depression + R wave elevation + T wave upright) | RCA or LCX |

**Interpretation points**: When abnormalities such as ST-segment elevation, pathological Q wave, T wave inversion, etc. occur in any area, the above "same group of leads" should be checked at the same time to see if they are consistent (only if there are ≥ 2 adjacent consecutive abnormal leads that have pathological significance), and whether there are mirror changes in the electrical contralateral leads to improve positioning accuracy and eliminate single-lead artifacts or normal variation interference.

## 8. Classification of each waveform form of the original image (summary review)

<a id="p波"></a>
### P wave
- Normal P wave: gentle hilly positive wave
- Pulmonary P wave (II, III, aVF)/right cardiac P wave (V1, V2): high peak
- Mitral P wave (I, II)/Levocardial P wave (P terminal force of V1 > 0.04mm·sec): bimodal notch

<a id="qrs波形态"></a>
### QRS wave shape
qR, RS, QS, QR, rS, rSR' (see naming rules in Section 2.1)

<a id="st段四种形态"></a>
### Four forms of ST segment
J type, slowly ascending type (SU type), horizontal type (H type), downward sloping type (DS type)

<a id="t波按对称性极性分类"></a>
### T wave (classified by symmetry and polarity)
Normal, sharp angle of ST-T (suspected of hemorrhagic heart disease), テント-like T (hyperaemia), ストレインパターン (cardiac obesity Large), coronary T (myocardial infarction), giant negative T wave (apical hypertrophy of myocardium), QT prolongation with T wave (non-Q wave infarction/internal hemorrhage)

<a id="u波"></a>
### U wave
Positive U wave, negative U wave (see Judgment Criteria in Section 6)
