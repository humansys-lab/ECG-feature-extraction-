# Concise 12-Lead ECG Diagnostic Reference

This reference supports hypothesis planning only. Patient-specific conclusions must be established from ecgfeat measurements acquired in the current run.

## Systematic interpretation

Review technical quality, rate, rhythm, atrial activity, AV association, PR/QRS/QT intervals, axes, conduction, ectopy, chamber/voltage patterns, Q waves, R-wave progression, ST-T-U morphology and pacing. Keep rate phenotypes separate from rhythm mechanisms.

## Rhythm and atrial mechanisms

A sinus mechanism requires organized atrial activation with compatible P-wave morphology and P-QRS association. Bradycardia or tachycardia alone does not establish sinus origin. Atrial fibrillation requires an irregular ventricular response plus lack of consistent organized P waves; candidate fibrillatory-wave fields from one detector chain are not independent confirmation. Atrial flutter requires repeated organized atrial activity with cross-lead support and a compatible AV relationship. Candidate atrial-event counts must not be converted into an AV conduction ratio without localized atrial depolarizations.

## AV and intraventricular conduction

First-degree AV delay requires a reportable prolonged PR interval and stable one-to-one AV conduction. Advanced AV block requires repeated reliable nonconducted atrial events and a reproducible sequential conduction pattern. A single detector event is insufficient. Bundle branch and fascicular block require both compatible QRS duration and diagnostic cross-lead morphology. Wide QRS alone supports only a nonspecific conduction phenotype after pacing and pre-excitation are considered.

## Pre-excitation and ectopy

Ventricular pre-excitation requires independent PR-shortening evidence and compatible delta/QRS-onset morphology across leads. Delta candidates alone are insufficient. Premature complexes require true prematurity in beat timing, compatible ectopic morphology and representative or repeated support; a single nondominant morphology group may be artifact or an outlier.

## Voltage, ischemia and repolarization

Voltage criteria are measured ECG phenotypes, not proof of anatomic hypertrophy. Interpret them cautiously or not at all with ventricular pacing or substantial QRS widening. ST and T-wave conclusions require reliable cross-lead distribution and review of secondary QRS causes. Q waves and abnormal R-wave progression require native-beat confirmation across contiguous leads and review of lead placement and conduction confounders.

## Interval interpretation

Use only reportable QT/QTc and PR measurements for definition-level interval conclusions. If an interval is unavailable, review its component waveform: P-wave visibility and AV association for PR, or T/U morphology and endpoint reliability for QT. Missing intervals neither confirm nor exclude disease.
